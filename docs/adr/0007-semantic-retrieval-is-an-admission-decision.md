# Semantic retrieval is an admission decision, and the two lanes that make it do not share a threshold

The embedding channel has been in this repository since ADR-0004 and has never done what it was added to do. The diagnosis is one sentence: **the embedding never failed — the architecture never listened to it (a ranking system with no admission gate), and it was being fed shredded paper the whole time (the FTS corpus defect, fixed by `SEMANTIC_CORPUS_VERSION` `"1"` → `"2"`).** Both halves are now measured rather than asserted, and this ADR records what the measurement says and what follows from it.

**It was being fed shredded paper.** Until the corpus join was fixed, `embeddable_revisions` indexed `context_fts` — `normalize_search_text` output, which for CJK is overlapping bigram shrapnel — while every calibration in the test suite encoded revision fields as written. So each floor cut one distribution in the suite and a different one in the field, and no test could see the gap, because the fixtures and the probes both took the intended path and only the backfill took the wrong one. The cost is now quantified: over the same real pair set, the raw-text space separates same-topic from cross-topic Contexts with an overlap of 425 basis points, the `context_fts` space with an overlap of 1259, and the best achievable threshold degrades from 3 false negatives and 0 false positives to 4 and 4. Roughly a third of the separation the encoder was capable of was being destroyed before it reached the comparison.

**The architecture never listened to it.** The channel has always ranked well and admitted badly, and those are different properties that one number was being asked to carry. On the clean-space calibration fixture, every Working Intent's best on-topic Context beats its best off-topic one by 2902 to 5584 basis points without exception — the ordering is essentially perfect — while the query floor of 2800 admits 31 of 96 off-topic Contexts, 32.3%. Ranking is not the failure; admission is. A Context that enters the channel is injected without any further test of relevance, so a floor that lets a third of an unrelated corpus in is not a weak filter, it is the absence of one, and no value of that constant converts a ranking signal into an admission decision. The device evidence is worse than the fixture's: five real Working Intents scored against a real installation's Contexts took their top hit from an unrelated topic every time, the highest at 5501 — inside the band this repository would have to admit.

**What separates cleanly is Context against Context.** The measurement that motivates the redesign is the symmetric one. Take a Context the Session already touched as a seed and compare it document-against-document with each candidate, and the four pair groups — same-repo/same-topic, cross-repo/same-topic, same-repo/cross-topic, cross-repo/cross-topic — separate at AUC 0.9981 on the real corpus (83/66/122/54 pairs, two checkouts) with a zero-false-positive band at (5130, 5190) and a best threshold of 5140 giving 100% precision at 95.5% cross-repository recall, on a stable 5000–5500 plateau. Notably, cross-repo/same-topic and same-repo/same-topic distributions almost coincide: the cross-repository join — the Android Context that explains the field the web Context consumes — is not the hard case, because both sides quote the same server contract. The hard case is same-repo/cross-topic, where two Contexts share the product's entire vocabulary (the word 搜索 and the card/module/default-value language around it) while describing unrelated work, and that group tops out at 5130 against cross-repo/cross-topic's 4555.

**So the retrieval becomes two lanes with two floors, and they are not two opinions about one boundary.** Lane B's second hop is an *admission*: seed Context against candidate Context, both encoded through the document path, governed by `SEMANTIC_HOP2_ADMISSION_FLOOR_BASIS_POINTS` = 5200. The query-side floor, `QWEN3_SEMANTIC_SIMILARITY_FLOOR_BASIS_POINTS` = 2800, belongs to neither lane and keeps exactly one job: explicit `context_search`, where a caller asked for a broad list and the query is a short human phrase. Intent-text semantic matching is retired from automatic injection — not because 2800 is the wrong number for it, but because it is the wrong mechanism for it. The two floors cut two different distributions for two different callers: the query calibration measured positives running 2403–8588 with the main mass starting at 3217, so a floor anywhere near 5200 would refuse most of what an explicit search exists to return, while 2800 on the document side would admit a third of any corpus.

**The second-hop floor is the union of two corpora, not the better of them.** `fixtures/association/hard-negative-v1.json` is built to be harder than the field: three topic families across two repository labels, deliberately sharing domain vocabulary across topics, with cross-cutting knowledge (a contrast-ratio finding) and entity-free validations (procedural prose with no identifier) planted as known hard cases. Its cross-topic ceiling is 5045 and 5200 clears it by 155 basis points at 0 of 191 false positives. 5140 would clear this fixture comfortably but sits 10 basis points above the *device's* ceiling, which is a reading of one pair rather than a threshold; 5100 would clear this fixture and admit the device's hardest cross-topic pair outright. 5200 is above both, and erring high is the standing rule — a Context nobody asked for is worse than no Context. Recall differs sharply between the two corpora (95.5% on the device, 28% here) and that difference is a property of the corpora: the device's same-topic family was one week of one feature's work, so its Contexts repeat each other's field names, while this fixture's families span a topic's whole history. The pessimistic number is the one kept in the test, so the floor is never tuned against an easy positive set.

**Pre-registered recalibration.** One synthetic corpus cross-checked against one installation is one installation more than any floor in this file previously had, and still not a distribution. Before the second hop governs injections in the field, the floor becomes a configuration key and each admission decision records its score, and the value is re-derived from that record once real traffic has accumulated — the discipline ADR-0004 wrote for the encode budget after setting it twice from the wrong machine. Three known limits of the device run are what to close first: its positives were single-topic (one feature family), its iOS checkout held no Contexts at all so the cross-repository claim rests on two repositories rather than three, and its known false negatives — cross-cutting knowledge and entity-free validation — are exactly the Context shapes a cosine over statement text cannot reach, which is a retrieval-signal question rather than a threshold question.

## Considered options

- Raising `QWEN3_SEMANTIC_SIMILARITY_FLOOR_BASIS_POINTS` until the intent path stops admitting off-topic Contexts was rejected, and is the option this ADR exists to refuse. The floor that would do it is the document-side one, and applying it to queries refuses most of what explicit `context_search` returns — the calibration's own positives start at 2403. The intent path's defect is that a ranking signal is being read as an admission decision; it is repaired by removing that path from automatic injection, not by moving its number.
- Sharing one constant between the two lanes, on the grounds that both are cosines in the same embedding space, was rejected. They are cosines over different *populations*: a Working Intent encoded through the query path with an instruction prefix against a document, versus two documents. The same space yields different distributions for the two, and this repository has twice paid for a retrieval constant transported between measurement conditions (ADR-0004's two encode-budget revisions). That `SEMANTIC_HOP2_ADMISSION_FLOOR_BASIS_POINTS` and bge-m3's `SEMANTIC_SIMILARITY_FLOOR_BASIS_POINTS` happen to both read 5200 is a coincidence of unrelated measurements and must not be read as convergence.
- Setting the second-hop floor at the device-derived 5140 was rejected as an overfit in the direction the repository has already named: 10 basis points above the measured ceiling is a reading of the single hardest pair. The synthetic fixture exists precisely so the value is not defended by one row of one corpus.
- Deriving the second-hop floor from the fixture alone (5100, the lowest round hundred above its own ceiling) was rejected because it would admit the device corpus's hardest cross-topic pair. A synthetic corpus can be made harder than the field on the negative side and still be easier on one particular pair; taking the union of both ceilings is the only reading that is correct on both.
- Keeping the intent path with a re-ranking stage instead of removing it was considered and deferred. It would treat the symptom the measurement actually shows — good ordering, bad admission — but it adds a second model-dependent stage to a channel whose first stage has never been validated in the field, and the two-lane design reaches the same Contexts through the seed hop with a threshold that is measured on both corpora.
- Treating the corpus-join fix as sufficient on its own was rejected. It was necessary — every number in this ADR is meaningless in the `context_fts` space — but the intent path's top hit was off-topic on the device run *in the clean space too*. Fixing what the encoder is fed does not make a ranking signal into a gate.
- Leaving `SEMANTIC_CORPUS_VERSION` at `"1"` and calibrating against the shipped `context_fts` space, on the grounds that calibrating where it runs is ADR-0004's own rule, was rejected. The rule says to calibrate in the space production uses; it does not say to keep a space that was never intended. The shrapnel corpus was a join defect, not a design, and the fixture text and the indexed text agreeing is what makes any calibration in this suite transferable at all.
